Scheduling behavior is installation-local and governed by the applicable configured service policy. The initial product scope uses configurable recurring operating periods and explicitly dated exceptions; one configurable customer appointment duration per service; and separate applicable preparation and cleanup buffers. Buffers reserve applicable staff/resource time separately from the customer's appointment duration. A dated exception applies only to its configured dates and periods. The effective schedule must reject ambiguous or conflicting date/period configuration and must not represent affected time as available. The system remains accessible when a schedule is invalid or unavailable, and correcting schedule configuration does not automatically cancel accepted appointments. It supports bounded combinations of staff and physical-resource requirements, including staff-only, resource-only, both, or neither when the approved service/capacity policy permits. The applicable service/time-period capacity and resource constraints must be respected together; they are distinct constraints, not interchangeable limits. Duplicate policy also applies according to its approved configuration. Detailed policy values and implementation mechanisms remain open.

Duplicate handling follows the configured client policy, comparing the approved attendee/eligibility criterion with the same service over its configured comparison window. The comparison window may differ by service. A booker-only match is not a duplicate criterion. In warning mode, an ordinary user may continue only where effective approved policy permits it and after an explicit, accessible acknowledgement; acknowledgement does not bypass other checks. In blocking mode, an ordinary user cannot bypass a prohibited duplicate. A permitted exception requires an actor with the specific applicable permission, an approved policy basis, a reason, and attributable evidence. Neither mode bypasses unrelated eligibility, capacity, resource, or authorization rules. Legitimate proxy and multiple-attendee cases must not be mistaken for duplicates solely because the same booker is involved. Detailed attendee criteria, service values, comparison windows and permitted exceptions remain open. Final duplicate and capacity decisions belong to the shared integrity boundary, not to a display calculation.
