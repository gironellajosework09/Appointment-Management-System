## Receptionist Interview  REC-001

### Direct Interview Notes and Responses

- **Interview date:** October 28, 2026
- **Participant reference:** REC-001
- **Role / organization type:** Receptionist / Front Desk Staff (Multi-Services Office)
- **Interview method:** Direct In-Person Interview
- **Permission to take notes:** Granted
- **Actual duration:** 24 minutes

#### Interview Responses

##### A. Opening Script

> *"Sure, take all the notes you need! Honestly, anything that helps fix our booking headaches is fine by me."*

##### B. Participant Background

**1. Role and Experience**

I’ve been working at the front desk here for about three years now. My main job is greeting visitors, handling walk-ins, answering the phone, and keeping track of who is coming in to see our staff.

**2. Daily Volume**

On an average day, we see around 40 to 60 scheduled people. Plus, we easily get another 10 to 15 people who just walk in off the street asking questions or trying to get seen on the spot.

**3. Current System**

It’s a mix of things, to be honest. We have a shared Google Sheet where we write down the appointments, but we also use Google Calendar to block off time. Then, on top of that, I keep a physical paper logbook right here on my desk so people can sign their names when they walk through the door.

##### C. Daily Appointment Workflow

**1. Walkthrough of a recent appointment**

Yesterday morning, a woman called asking for a 10:00 AM slot today. I opened up our calendar, saw 10:00 AM was open, took her name and phone number, and typed it into the spreadsheet. When she walked in this morning, I asked her name, checked my list, had her sign the paper logbook, and then pinged the officer on Slack to let him know his 10:00 AM was sitting in the lobby.

**2. Determining availability**

We just look at the shared Google Calendar. If a time slot doesn’t have anything typed in it between 8:00 AM and 5:00 PM, we consider it open. Staff are supposed to block off their own lunch hours or out-of-office meetings, but sometimes they forget.

**3. Double-bookings and full days**

If two people try to grab the same slot—say someone books online or another receptionist takes a call at the exact same time—whoever typed it in first gets it. If a day is totally full, we tell the caller we’re maxed out and try to push them to tomorrow or next week. If it’s really urgent, I write their name down on a sticky note as a back-up in case someone cancels.

**4. Creating appointments for customers**

Oh, all the time. A lot of our older clients or people who call in on the phone don't want to use a website or don't know how. So we take their details over the phone or at the counter and put it into the system for them.

- **Follow-up details:**
- *Who changes availability:* Admin supervisors can change operating hours, but staff usually just block off their own schedules.
- *Appointment types:* Yes, quick consultations are 15 minutes, standard visits are 30 minutes, and full file reviews take an hour.
- *Capacity exceptions:* If a boss or manager walks over and says, *"Hey, squeeze this person in,"* we have to make an exception and overlap the schedule, even if we're full.

##### D. Changes, Cancellations, and Exceptions

**1. Rescheduling and Cancellations**

When someone calls to cancel, I go into the Google Sheet and delete or highlight their line, then add whoever was next on my manual waiting list. The annoying part is when people just don't call at all. Last-minute cancellations leave us sitting there with a 30-minute gap where no one gets served.

**2. Late arrivals, no-shows, walk-ins**

- **Late arrivals:** We usually give people a 15-minute buffer. If they show up 20 or 30 minutes late, their spot might already be given to a walk-in, so they have to sit and wait until there’s an opening.
- **No-shows:** If it’s 30 minutes past their time and they haven't called, we mark them as "No-Show."
- **Walk-ins:** We tell them upfront that scheduled appointments come first. They can take a seat in the waiting area, and if someone fails to show up, we call them up next.

**3. A recent difficult situation**

Just last week, another receptionist and I were both booking 2:00 PM slots at the exact same time. Her spreadsheet hadn't refreshed on my screen yet, so we both confirmed two different clients for 2:00 PM. Both people walked in at 1:55 PM expecting to go straight in.

- **How it was handled:** I had to apologize to both of them. One guy came from two hours away, so we prioritized him. The other person was understandably upset, so I got them a glass of water, apologized again, and talked to the officer to see if he could work through his break to take her at 2:30 PM.

##### E. Arrival and Verification

**1. Confirming valid appointments**

When someone walks up, they usually give their name or show me the confirmation text/email on their phone. I match that name with my printed daily list or the Google Sheet on my screen.

**2. Tracking client statuses**

We use a simple color code on our spreadsheet:

- **Yellow:** Expected today.
- **Green:** Sitting in the waiting room.
- **Blue:** Inside the office with the officer.
- **Grey:** Done and left.
- **Red:** Didn't show up.

**3. Mixed up or duplicate details**

It happens a lot. People show up on Tuesday thinking their appointment was Tuesday, but when I check, it was actually scheduled for Thursday. Or a wife shows up using her husband’s name. I usually just ask for a government ID or search their phone number in our records to find out what happened.

##### F. System Problems and Recovery

**1. System or internet outages**

When the Wi-Fi drops or Google Sheets won't load, we rely on the paper printout. Every morning at 8:00 AM, the first thing I do is print out the entire day's schedule on paper. If the internet dies, I pull out that piece of paper and track everyone with a pen.

**2. Post-outage recovery**

Once the connection comes back, I have to go back to my computer and manually type in who actually arrived, who was late, and who didn't show up based on my handwritten paper notes. It takes extra time, but it keeps the records accurate.

##### G. Priorities and Closing

**1. Biggest frustration**

Dealing with double-bookings because the system didn't update fast enough, and spending half my day answering the phone just to tell people whether we have open spots on Friday.

**2. The single biggest improvement needed**

A system that updates in real-time so two people can't book the exact same minute, combined with automatic text messages that remind clients about their appointment so they stop forgetting to show up.

**3. Final thoughts**

Text reminders are huge. Most of our no-shows aren't doing it to be rude; they simply forgot they booked it three weeks ago. If they got a text saying *"Reply 1 to Confirm or 2 to Cancel,"* it would save us hours of wasted time every single week.

#### AI-Assisted Response Reconstruction

##### A. Business and Workflow

**Q1. Can you describe your business and how appointments are currently handled?**

> *"We run a full-service salon and spa with about six regular staff on the floor. Honestly, 90% of our bookings happen through Facebook Messenger and Viber. People message our page asking for open slots. My front desk receptionist handles the chats, checks our shared Google Calendar, and writes things down in a physical logbook just to be sure. If it's a major hair treatment or a weekend package, we usually ask them to send a 30% deposit via GCash first so they don't ghost us."*

**Q2. Think about a recent busy day. What happened from the first appointment request until the customer finished their visit?**

> *"Oh, last Saturday was chaotic. We had around 30 scheduled clients, plus a handful of walk-ins. The morning started with three different people messaging Messenger at the same time while the phone was ringing. My receptionist was scrambling to reply to chats, take calls, and greet people walking through the door. A client arrived for a 10:00 AM slot, but because my staff were swamped, she had to wait 15 minutes before a chair opened up. The staff spent half their day jumping between the front desk, the chat inbox, and actual service work."*

##### B. Scheduling and Capacity Policies

**Q3. How do you determine how many appointments your business can accept each day?**

> *"It really depends on our available staff and equipment. We have six salon chairs, three massage beds, and a specific number of stylists and therapists. A basic haircut takes 30 minutes, but a full rebond or hair color takes 3 to 4 hours. We have to factor in preparation and clean-up time between treatments, otherwise everything gets delayed by the afternoon."*

**Q4. Tell me about the last time your schedule became overbooked or two appointments conflicted.**

> *"That happened about two weeks ago. One receptionist booked a regular client for a hair treatment via phone call, while another staff member accepted a Messenger booking for that exact same chair and time slot. When both clients showed up at 2:00 PM, my receptionist panicked. I had to step in, apologize profusely, offer one of them a complimentary scalp treatment while they waited, and reorganize our schedule."*

**Q5. Have you ever accepted an additional appointment when the schedule was already full?**

> *"Yes, all the time, but strictly for VIP regulars or emergency requests—like someone attending a wedding who suddenly needs styling. Usually, my front desk can't approve that; they have to ask me first. If I allow it, we make an exception, squeeze them in, and note it down as a manual override on our calendar."*

##### C. Cancellations, Late Arrivals, and Waitlists

**Q6. Walk me through the last appointment that was cancelled or rescheduled.**

> *"Just yesterday, a client messaged on Viber at 11:00 AM saying she couldn't make her 2:00 PM slot because of work meetings. Since we didn't have an automated waitlist, my receptionist had to manually scroll through recent chat messages to find someone who wanted an afternoon slot. Luckily, a regular client who lived nearby took it on short notice. If we hadn't filled it, we would've lost that revenue, and our GCash deposit policy can be tricky to enforce without getting into arguments."*

**Q7. What happens when a customer arrives late or does not arrive at all?**

> *"We give them a 15-minute grace period. If they're 20 minutes late without texting us, we give their slot to a walk-in, and if they finally show up later, they have to wait for an open gap. If they completely ghost us and don't reply to our messages, we mark them as a no-show. For first-timers, we let it go with a warning; for repeat offenders, we require full advance payment next time."*

##### D. Staff Permissions and Verification

**Q8. Which appointment-related decisions can employees make independently, and which require your approval?**

> *"My staff can book, reschedule within normal limits, and handle standard walk-ins. But anything involving refunds, waiving no-show fees, giving special discounts ('paki-bigyan naman ng discount si kumare'), or overbooking a fully booked day requires my direct approval."*

**Q9. How do employees confirm that a customer has a valid appointment when they arrive?**

> *"Usually, they just ask the customer's name and check our Google Calendar or the morning printout. Sometimes clients show a screenshot of their Messenger confirmation. Occasionally, someone shows up on the wrong day or forgets their schedule, and we have to dig through our chat history to straighten it out."*

**Q10. Think about the busiest arrival period you remember. What made check-in difficult?**

> *"During peak hours, everyone arrives around the same time—like Saturday afternoons. When three people walk in simultaneously, the receptionist gets overwhelmed trying to figure out who has an appointment, who is a walk-in, and who is just picking up a product. We don't have a digital queue board, so staff rely on memory or shouting across the room to ask who is next."*

##### E. Outages and Operational Continuity

**Q11. Have you experienced an internet outage, power interruption, or booking-system failure during operating hours?**

> *"All the time—especially during typhoon season or when local brownouts happen. When the Wi-Fi drops, Google Sheets won't load, and Messenger stops working. To prevent total chaos, my receptionist prints out a hard copy of the day's schedule every single morning at 8:00 AM. When the internet goes down, we switch to pen and paper. Once power and internet return, we manually type everything back into the computer."*

##### F. Notifications, Costs, and Business Priorities

**Q12. How do you currently remind customers about their appointments?**

> *"We manually send a Messenger or Viber reminder the afternoon before their visit. It works well because Filipinos check Messenger constantly, but doing it manually takes up a lot of my receptionist's time. If we forget to send it, our no-show rate jumps up immediately."*

**Q13. What does your current appointment-management process cost your business?**

> *"I haven't calculated down to the exact centavo, but the hidden costs are real: at least an hour or two of staff time wasted replying to repetitive chat inquiries, mobile load for texting, GCash transfer fees, and lost revenue whenever someone no-shows and we can't fill their spot."*

**Q14. If you could improve just one part of your appointment process, what would you change first, and why?**

> *"Definitely a system that stops double-bookings and automatically sends reminders so people stop forgetting. If we could eliminate double-bookings and cut our no-shows in half, that alone would boost our weekly revenue and save my staff so much daily stress."*

##### G. Closing

**Q15. Is there anything important about managing appointments that I haven't asked?**

> *"Just that Filipinos love flexibility. Clients will often message saying, 'Pwede bang ma-move ng 30 mins?' or book through a relative or spouse. A rigid, overly robotic booking system wouldn't fit how local customers actually behave—we need something structured in the backend, but flexible enough for us to manage human quirks on the floor."*

#### AI-Assisted Response Reconstruction

##### A. Office Background and Workflow

**Q1. Can you describe your services and how clients schedule appointments?**

> *"We handle public applications, document authentications, and consultations. Honestly, it’s a mix. For simple document requests or inquiries, people can just walk in, get a number from our queuing machine, and wait. But for interviews or complex evaluations, we require an appointment. Clients usually book through our online web portal, but a lot of them still call our landline trunkline, email us, or message our official Facebook page. Sometimes, an anak or relative will book on behalf of an elderly parent because the parent doesn't know how to use a smartphone."*

**Q2. Can you walk me through a typical busy day?**

> *"Naku, malaking gulo kapag peak hours! We open at 8:00 AM, but people start lining up outside as early as 6:30 AM. My frontline staff check the morning appointment list while simultaneously setting up the physical queue. By 8:30 AM, you have scheduled clients, walk-ins, senior citizens, PWDs, pregnant women, and people referred from other agencies all mixed together in the lobby.*

> *The receptionist has to verify their documents first. If a requirement is missing—like a missing ID or wrong document format—we can't process them, which leads to arguments. The most time-consuming part isn't even the actual service; it's explaining why a document is rejected, handling walk-ins who insist on being squeezed in, and answering repetitive questions. Delays always happen when a client brings incomplete papers or when the approving officer gets pulled into an unexpected meeting."*

##### B. Scheduling, Capacity, and Eligibility

**Q3. How does your office determine the number of appointments it can accommodate?**

> *"It depends heavily on the service type. For a simple document release, we can take up to 60 people a day. But for detailed legal consultations or technical assessments handled by specific officers, we cap it at 15 because each session takes 45 minutes to an hour. We look at available personnel, working computer terminals, open windows, and official operating hours. We also have to reserve capacity for our mandatory priority lanes."*

**Q4. What happens when appointment requests exceed your capacity?**

> *"Normally, our system or our staff tells the client to pick the next available date. But in reality, people come all the way from the provinces—say, from Ilocos Sur or Abra—and they tell us, 'Ma'am, malayo po biyahe namin.' In those urgent cases, my staff will endorse the client to me, and I have to evaluate whether we can make an exception. If our officers are completely packed, we place them on a manual waitlist or advise them to check our portal for cancelled slots, though that rarely works smoothly."*

**Q5. Are there eligibility requirements that clients must satisfy before booking or receiving a service?**

> *"Yes, absolutely. They need a valid government ID, filled-out application forms, and specific supporting documents listed in our Citizen's Charter. If they're sending a representative, we strictly require an authorization letter, a copy of the principal's ID, and the representative's own ID. If their documents are incomplete, my staff cannot proceed. We have to turn them away or ask them to secure the missing paper first, which makes clients very upset."*

##### C. Cancellations, Rescheduling, and Attendance

**Q6. Tell me about a recent appointment that a client cancelled or requested to reschedule.**

> *"Just last week, a guy booked an appointment but called us saying he couldn't make it because of a sudden work emergency. Our online portal is a bit rigid—once an appointment is cancelled, it's completely gone from the active schedule; it doesn't automatically bounce back to a public pool. So my staff had to manually call someone from our makeshift paper waitlist to take that 2:00 PM slot. It’s a very manual, trial-and-error process."*

**Q7. How do you handle clients who arrive late, miss their appointment, or arrive without one?**

> *"We give a 15-minute grace period because of traffic and public transport issues here. If they arrive 30 minutes late, they lose their priority, and we slot them into the walk-in queue. If they don't show up at all, we tag them as a 'No-Show' at the end of the day. For walk-ins without appointments, we accommodate them only if a scheduled client cancels or if we have empty gaps between appointments. Priority clients like senior citizens always get pushed to the front, of course."*

##### D. Appointment Verification and Client Check-In

**Q8. When a client arrives, how do staff determine whether that person has a valid appointment?**

> *"They show us their printed confirmation or the reference code / QR code on their smartphone screen. My staff cross-checks that code against our master daily list on the computer. If an older client forgot their code or has no internet to open their email, we search our database using their full name and birthdate. If we truly can't find a record, we treat them as a walk-in if our daily quota allows."*

**Q9. What problems have occurred when verifying appointments or recording client arrivals?**

> *"Oh, marami! People screenshot old confirmations with the wrong dates, or they accidentally book twice because they thought the first booking didn't go through, which creates duplicate records. Sometimes a wife arrives bringing documents for her husband without proper authorization, and we have to argue about agency rules. And during rush hour, when lines are long, a stressed receptionist might accidentally click the wrong name on the screen and mark the wrong person as 'Arrived'."*

**Q10. After confirming an appointment, how do you record that a client has arrived and eventually received the service?**

> *"We track it in stages on our internal system, though during heavy rushes, staff scribble it on paper first. We use statuses like: **Booked, Arrived (Waiting), In-Service (Window 3), Completed, Incomplete Requirements,** and **No-Show**. Keeping these distinct is crucial because a person can be physically sitting in the lobby, but if their documents are incomplete, they aren't actually 'ready for service'."*

##### E. Staff Authority, Privacy, and Accountability

**Q11. Which appointment-related actions can ordinary staff perform, and which require supervisory approval?**

> *"Frontline staff can look up records, issue queue numbers, verify documents, and mark attendance. But they cannot delete a record, change an official appointment date, override a daily quota, or excuse an incomplete requirement. All of those require my sign-off as the supervisor. We need strict accountability—if an appointment is moved or modified, the system must log who made the change so there's no fixing things behind the scenes."*

**Q12. What difficulties does your office encounter when protecting client information while allowing staff to perform their work?**

> *"Balancing data privacy with speed is tough. Our staff share terminals or use a common email inbox for bookings, which makes strict individual accountability hard to maintain under pressure. Also, under the Data Privacy Act, we cannot just give out client details to a relative who walks in asking about someone else's appointment unless there's a notarized SPA or clear written authorization. People get offended by that, but we have strict legal obligations."*

##### F. Outages and Operational Continuity

**Q13. What happens when your office experiences an internet outage, power interruption, or system failure?**

> *"Madalas ito, lalo na kapag umuulan ng malakas o nagkakaroon ng brownout sa area namin. When the internet drops, our online booking verification stops working completely. To survive this, our golden rule is that every morning at 7:30 AM, my staff prints out the complete daily appointment manifest on paper.

> When the system dies, we instantly switch to pen and paper. We check IDs against that printed paper list and write down walk-ins manually. Once the power and internet come back up late in the afternoon, my poor staff have to sit down and manually reconcile all the handwritten notes back into the database. It’s tedious, but it’s the only way public service doesn't grind to a complete halt."*

##### G. Communication, Accessibility, and Priorities

**Q14. How are clients informed about their appointment dates, schedule changes, or requirements?**

> *"They get an automated email or SMS when they finish booking. If there's a sudden office closure—like during a typhoon or a suspension declared by Malacañang—we post urgent advisories on our official Facebook page, and our staff try to text affected clients as fast as possible.

> The biggest challenge is that not everyone has a smartphone or reliable mobile data. Many citizens from lower-income or rural areas rely on a neighbor or relative's phone, or they just show up blindly as walk-ins. We have to design our frontline process to accommodate these people without totally breaking the appointment system."*

**Q15. If you could improve one part of your office's appointment process, what would you prioritize, and why?**

> *"I would prioritize a bulletproof, real-time tracking dashboard that integrates walk-ins and scheduled appointments into one seamless queue, combined with a reliable offline backup mode. If my staff could see at a glance who has arrived, whose documents are pending, and who didn't show up—without crashing when the internet flickers—it would cut our lobby wait times in half and eliminate 80% of our daily stress."*

### Closing Question

**Q16. Is there anything important about appointment management in your office that I haven't asked?**

> *"Just remember that government work isn't like a commercial spa or a private clinic. We cannot simply turn people away or say 'Fully booked na po, uwi na kayo' when someone traveled five hours to get here. A government appointment system must be structured enough to maintain order, but flexible enough to handle human realities, walk-in hardships, and connectivity dropouts."*

## Product Strategy Analysis

### 1. Should walk-in queue management be in the initial product?

**My take:** **Yes, but keep it lightweight.** Don't try to build a massive, multi-screen airport terminal system on day one.

In the Philippines, you simply cannot ignore walk-ins. Whether it’s a government office bound by public service rules or a salon that doesn't want to turn away cash in hand, walk-ins happen every single day. If our app only manages scheduled appointments and completely ignores the 15 people sitting on plastic chairs in the lobby, we're only solving half the receptionist's problem.

- **What we should build for MVP:** A simple screen where the receptionist can hit *"Add Walk-In"*, type a name and service, print or text a basic queue number, and drop them into a unified list alongside the scheduled appointments.
- **What we should defer:** Kiosk hardware integrations, fancy TV display boards, or complex multi-counter routing algorithms.
> **The key question we still need to answer:** *"When a walk-in arrives, do they take an empty appointment slot on the main calendar, or do they wait in a completely separate 'overflow' line that staff only call when they have free time?"*

### 2. Which eligibility checks are essential versus organization-specific?

**My take:** **Never hard-code eligibility rules into the core software.**

Every organization has its own quirks. A salon might just need to know if you're getting a hair color or a haircut. A dental clinic needs your medical history and insurance. A government office needs three government IDs, a barangay clearance, and a brown envelope. If we try to hard-code those rules, we'll spend forever rewriting code for every new client.

- **What the system should actually do:** Build a flexible **Checklist Builder**. Let the business or agency create their own list of required documents or check-boxes per service (e.g., [ ] Valid Government ID, [ ] Authorization Letter, [ ] Downpayment Received).
- **Staff control:** Give the receptionist a clear set of buttons on arrival: **Complete**, **Incomplete / Returned**, or **Needs Supervisor Review**.
> **The key question we still need to answer:** *"If a client arrives with missing papers, do they get turned away immediately, or do staff let them sit in the lobby while someone emails or brings the missing document?"*

### 3. When can another person book or attend on a customer's behalf?

**My take:** **We have to support proxy bookings from day one, but we must keep an audit trail.**

Let's be real—in Philippine culture, proxy booking is everywhere. An OFW working in Dubai books an appointment for their elderly mother in Pangasinan. A wife books a dental visit for her husband. An executive assistant books a medical check-up for their boss. If our system assumes that *the person holding the phone* is *the person sitting in the chair*, it will break immediately.

- **How the data model should look:**
1. **Booked By:** (e.g., Maria Santos, Daughter)
2. **Actual Client / Attendee:** (e.g., Juan Santos, Father)
3. **Verification Documents Needed:** (e.g., [ ] Authorization Letter attached, [ ] Principal ID presented)
> **The key question we still need to answer:** *"Which specific services allow a representative to complete the whole transaction alone, and which ones strictly require the actual owner to physically show up?"*

### 4. Does a validated QR code prove only the booking, or something more?

**My take:** **A QR code proves you have a ticket; it does NOT prove who you are.**

People misunderstand QR codes all the time. A QR code is just a fast way to type an appointment number into a computer. If I screenshot my QR code and text it to my cousin, my cousin now has my QR code. If the front desk scans it and automatically marks me as "Present," the system just lied.

- **What QR is good for:** Speed. The receptionist scans the code, and BAM—the client’s record pops up on screen instantly instead of the staff having to spell out *"G-I-R-O-N-E-L-L-A"* letter by letter.
- **What QR cannot replace:** The receptionist still needs to look at the person, look at their physical ID, and verify they are actually allowed to be there.
> **The key question we still need to answer:** *"Are our clients using QR codes mainly to speed up the line at the front desk, or are they expecting the QR code to act as a secure digital ID?"*

### 5. How should priority appointments and supervisor exceptions interact with capacity limits?

**My take:** **Never let the system quietly overbook itself, but ALWAYS give supervisors a 'Master Key' to bypass the limit.**

In a perfect world, a calendar has 20 slots, and when 20 slots are filled, the system shuts the door. But in the real world, a Senior Citizen walks in after traveling three hours on a bus, or the owner's friend needs an urgent haircut for a wedding. If the system completely blocks the staff with no way out, staff will simply stop using the system and go back to pen and paper.

- **The right workflow:**
1. Standard public booking hits the hard limit (e.g., 20/20 slots full) and stops.
2. If an exception happens, staff click **"Request Capacity Override."**
3. They select a reason: Senior Citizen / PWD Priority, Emergency / Urgent, or Owner / Supervisor Approval.
4. The system logs *who* approved it, *why*, and *when*, and puts an "Override" tag on that slot for reporting later.
> **The key question we still need to answer:** *"Can frontline staff perform an override on their own if they type in a valid reason, or must a supervisor physically enter a password to unlock the extra slot?"*

### 6. What minimum operations must remain possible during outages?

**My take:** **The absolute bare minimum is a daily morning printout and a manual check-in log.**

Typhoons happen, fiber cables get cut, and power grids fail. If our cloud-based appointment system dies when the Wi-Fi dies, the entire office grinds to a halt. We don't necessarily need a massive, offline-first database sync architecture for version 1, but we *must* support the real-world paper fallback that every single receptionist already uses.

- **The non-negotiable feature:** A **"Print Daily Roster"** button that staff press every morning at 7:30 AM.
- **The recovery feature:** When the internet comes back at 3:00 PM, a simple, fast "Reconciliation Screen" where the receptionist can quickly check off *Arrived*, *No-Show*, or *Completed* for the day based on their paper notes without having to click into 50 individual client profiles.
> **The key question we still need to answer:** *"During an outage, do staff only need to view who was supposed to come and mark who arrived, or do they also need the ability to create brand-new bookings while offline?"*
