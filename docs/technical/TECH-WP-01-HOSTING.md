# TECH-WP-01 D — Hosting and recoverability

**Status:** Comparative evaluation, 2026-11-13. Conditional recommendations only; no profile, provider, mechanism, objective or operational responsibility is approved. Current SDD-024 v0.2, SDD-025 v0.3 and SDD-026 v0.3 await exact-version review; prior approvals remain as recorded in the [acceptance matrix](../../TASKS.md#consolidated-exact-version-acceptance-matrix). D-083 permits both client and provider hosting; D-084 approves one standardized supported-profile principle, not a profile.

| Choice and candidates | Benefits and constraints | Failure, security/privacy and burden | Conditional recommendation and evidence needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Supported profile: client-operated standard environment vs provider-operated equivalent standard environment | Client custody may fit local control; provider operation may centralize expertise. Both require independent installations and compatible supported runtime, database and maintenance. A standardized profile can reduce support variants; it need not imply identical responsibility. | Client staffing gaps or provider outage can prevent recovery; privileged access and location create privacy exposure. Both entail lifecycle, patching, monitoring, licensing and support costs; actual terms and costs are unknown. | Compare a single documented technical profile across both models, conditional on actual platform compatibility, lifecycle, cost and security evidence. PO approves material profile; client/provider agree actual allocation. No OS, vendor or container selected. |
| Backup: client-controlled protected copies vs provider-managed protected copies | Client custody supports control; managed capability may simplify scheduling. Both need separation, integrity, access restrictions, key custody and lifecycle decisions. | Ransomware, lost keys, incomplete copies and unauthorized disclosure can defeat either. Independent copies and separation increase storage, staffing and key-management burden. | Compare protection and recoverability against a named installation's threat model and responsibilities; security/privacy review and PO material selection precede implementation. Obtain provider terms and full cost estimates before selection. |
| Restore verification: isolated synthetic exercise vs separately authorized real-backup verification | Synthetic exercises expose procedure and access issues with less sensitive data; a real backup can establish recovery of that particular copy when authorized. Neither generalizes automatically to every backup. | Synthetic data can miss real corruption; restored real data can leak or retain restricted/deleted information. Isolation, cleanup, access control and review impose operational cost. | Establish the procedure using authorized isolated data, then separately authorize privacy-safe verification of the relevant real backup before operational reliance as required by D-091. Record completeness, permissions, integrity, restricted/deleted data handling and elapsed recovery evidence; no test has occurred. |
| Rollback: revert application artifact/configuration vs restore a consistent recovery point | Artifact rollback may be faster when data changes remain compatible; restore may recover broader corruption but can lose later changes. | Incompatible data changes or partial rollback may worsen an incident; restored data and secrets require restricted access. Both require rehearsed ownership, version compatibility and recovery-point assessment. | Select an installation-specific strategy only after compatibility and failure analysis; rehearse under separate authorization. Escalate irreversible data changes and uncertain outcomes rather than promise rollback. |

**Failure and verification criteria:** For each proposed profile demonstrate, in a separately authorized environment, isolation, least privilege, monitoring and alert ownership, backup integrity, restore completeness, key recovery, recovery time and data loss measured against later approved objectives, and application/data compatibility after rollback. Simulate unavailable operator, compromised credentials, corrupt backup and provider outage; document recovery and residual risk. A plan, created backup, tested procedure and verified particular client backup are separate claims. No numerical RPO/RTO is approved under D-109.

**Decision and ownership:** `SECURITY/OPS-Q01` concerns operational ownership; `SDD-026/OPS-Q01` concerns the supported deployment profile. Assign named client/provider owners for custody, monitoring, incidents, restore, credentials and exceptional access per installation before reliance. Product Owner decides material profile and risk; engineering, security and privacy specialists review; contractual and processing terms require client/provider review. Default support remains non-data; exceptional access requires the separately governed authorization. PH-01–PH-10 remain pending qualified review.

**Evidence limits:** [SDD-025](../specs/SDD-025-BACKUP-RESTORATION-AND-RECOVERABILITY-EVIDENCE.md), [SDD-026](../specs/SDD-026-DEPLOYMENT-MAINTENANCE-AND-EXCEPTIONAL-SUPPORT-READINESS.md), [Security](../security/SECURITY.md) and [D-102](../decisions/DECISION_REGISTER.md) establish project constraints. [AWS Backup restore testing documentation](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/aws-backup/latest/devguide/restore-testing.html) illustrates periodic restore viability testing and its service-specific scope; it is not a provider recommendation or proof of iTakda recovery. Actual platform terms, supported combinations, costs and installation evidence remain unexamined.
