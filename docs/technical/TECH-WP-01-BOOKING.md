# TECH-WP-01 — Booking integrity evaluation (track A)

**Status: evaluation draft, 2026-11-13.** Engineering/architecture prepares this comparison; a qualified security/data-integrity reviewer and, where relevant, privacy reviewer must assess it. The Product Owner decides material scope and risk trade-offs. Named reviewers and evidence owners remain to be assigned. This is not a mechanism selection, specification approval, authorization to implement or test, or acceptance of risk. No application, database, load or security test was run for this evaluation.

## Decision context and constraints

The P0 queue in [TASKS.md](../../TASKS.md) calls for comparing transactions, constraints/locking and same-intent reconciliation, including failure interleavings and future verification. [D-102](../ENGINEERING_GOVERNANCE.md) requires benefits, alternatives, credible failures, recovery, verification and residual risk before implementation approval. Effective requirements are [v0.9](../requirements/REQUIREMENTS-SPECIFICATION-v0.9.md), read with [v0.8](../requirements/REQUIREMENTS-SPECIFICATION-v0.8.md) and unchanged v0.7 in [the preserved specification](../requirements/REQUIREMENTS_SPECIFICATION.md); traceability is [revision 1.3](../requirements/REQUIREMENTS-TRACEABILITY-v1.3.md) with its preserved predecessors. FR-002, NFR-006, BR-001/002/010/013 and AC-005/014/018/022/026 are particularly relevant. These are development-stage assumptions, not evidence of validation.

[SDD-006 v0.1](../specs/SDD-006-SHARED-BOOKING-INTEGRITY-AND-AUTHORITATIVE-OUTCOMES.md) is pending exact-version Product Owner review; [SDD-005 v0.4](../specs/SDD-005-AVAILABILITY-RESOURCE-CAPACITY-AND-SCHEDULING-POLICY.md) is also draft, while its v0.2 has an exact-scope historical approval. Neither approves a technical method. Decisions D-106/D-107 require both applicable capacity and resource constraints; exclusive resources cannot overlap and shared limits must be configured. The same approved attendee/eligibility criterion and service-specific window applies across paths; booker identity alone is insufficient. Permission-specific exceptions need a reason and attributable evidence, not a blanket bypass. D-108 requires applicable default-deny authority and independent appointment-scoped guest proof; a public reference alone is not authority.

Availability is advisory. Final decisions must use coherent effective policy and current state across guest, account, staff, proxy, walk-in and other authorized booking-affecting paths. A successful outcome and required evidence must agree; a known rejection leaves no partial or orphaned effects. A rejected reschedule preserves the original allocation; successful release and reallocation are one logical operation. Unknown is neither success nor rejection. Same-intent reconciliation must precede safe follow-up; delivery of email is independent of the committed booking. Configuration changes affecting accepted appointments require the separate SDD-022 impact boundary.

Capacity units, resource categories, matching criteria/windows, permitted exceptions, cutoff values and same-intent identity/retention remain unresolved (SDD-006 INT-Q01–Q05). Consequently no particular schema, key, isolation level, database, package, topology or policy value is assumed here. D-067's 20–100 appointments/day planning profile is not a concurrency ceiling. D-070/AC-005 requires a **future integrity verification**: 50 valid simultaneous requests for one remaining unit, exactly one reservation succeeds, the other 49 obtain safe clear unsuccessful outcomes, and no over-capacity, prohibited duplicate, partial or orphaned state exists. It is neither a measured result nor a latency or availability SLA; Q-016/Q-022 numeric targets remain open under D-109.

## Candidate comparison

These are composable approaches, not mutually exclusive full designs. All require a single authoritative boundary for every relevant write path and coherent policy/occupancy decisions. A constraint or lock on one capacity row alone does not establish correct resource overlap, duplicate-window, policy-change or reschedule behavior.

| Candidate | Benefits and conditions | Costs, risks and evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| Transaction with pessimistic locking of all relevant authoritative state | Can serialize competing decisions and rollback the complete booking, allocation and required evidence together, if the chosen store actually protects every affected predicate and all writers cooperate. Contention behavior is comparatively direct to reason about. | Lock scope/order across multiple resources, absent rows and ranges, policy revisions and duplicate windows is difficult; incomplete coverage permits races. Deadlocks, long waits and reduced throughput are credible. Requires store-specific isolation/lock documentation, a consistent acquisition strategy and interleaving tests; lock timeout alone is not proof of rejection. Operational burden: monitoring contention and handling stuck/failed transactions. |
| Transaction with optimistic version/compare-and-swap and retry | Shorter lock holding can suit low contention; conflicts can be detected when every relevant state and policy version is included in the atomic condition. | A single version may miss overlapping ranges or multiple independently changing resources; retries can starve under contention, and a retry after an uncertain commit can duplicate intent. Requires bounded conflict handling, a fresh full policy evaluation on a genuinely new attempt, and proof that all predicates participate atomically. Operational burden: conflict monitoring and careful version lifecycle. |
| Database-enforced uniqueness, exclusion or conditional constraints combined with an atomic transaction | Strong last line of defense for invariants the chosen store can express, including some unique references or non-overlap/uniqueness rules; protects against overlooked application writers when applicable. | Expressiveness and semantics vary by store; a fixed uniqueness rule may incorrectly prohibit permitted warning/proxy cases, and aggregate capacity or policy-dependent windows may not be representable. Constraint failures need truthful, non-disclosing mapping and rollback. Requires vendor-primary documentation for the eventual version and adversarial tests; constraints do not replace authorization or policy checks. Schema migration and diagnosis add burden. |
| Serializable transaction / predicate protection | In principle can prevent invalid interleavings even where a matching row does not yet exist, if the actual store's documented semantics cover the predicates and all writes use the boundary. | Implementations differ: aborts, retries, contention and phantom behavior must be understood, including configuration updates. An isolation label alone is not evidence; distributed/external effects may be outside its scope. Requires engine-specific review and tests of retries, policy races and resource ranges. |
| Single ordered command processor / queue | Can simplify ordering within a correctly defined conflict partition and provide controlled backpressure. | A single global queue reduces availability and throughput; partitioning by slot alone misses cross-slot resources, duplicates, rescheduling and configuration changes. Crash, redelivery, ordering and split-brain introduce durable ownership and deduplication burdens. Requires proof of exclusive authority, cross-partition atomicity and recovery, not merely FIFO claims. Adds operational infrastructure; no queue is selected. |

### Same-intent outcome approaches

| Candidate | Advantages and conditions | Risks, burden and outstanding evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Client-supplied opaque operation identifier with server-side durable outcome | Can correlate a repeated submission with its original intent across a lost response; an atomic association between intent and outcome can return the established result. | Requires collision/guessing resistance, scoped authorization, payload binding and defined concurrent duplicate handling; same identifier with different payload must not silently reuse an unrelated outcome. Retention and expiry determine whether safe reconciliation remains possible. Stores additional sensitive metadata and needs cleanup and recovery procedures. |
| Server-issued intent/receipt before mutation | Can bind a server-defined intent to a later authoritative result and limit client identifier assumptions. | Adds a round trip and a failure boundary before receipt delivery; a lost receipt, expiration, multiple receipt issuance or unauthorized lookup must not create duplicate commands or leak appointment existence. Requires durable association, scope and recovery design. |
| Authorized status lookup using existing appointment/business attributes, with manual assistance for ambiguity | May avoid a new client-managed token for some cases and offers a human recovery path. | An appointment reference or matching personal data alone does not establish authority or same intent; absence from a search is not proof of non-commit, and similar legitimate requests can be conflated. Manual handling adds staffing, delay and privacy risk; define escalation and evidence rather than assuming lookup always resolves ambiguity. |

For each approach distinguish the command identity from the appointment's public reference, bind an authorized claimant to the relevant operation, and define how a result can be found after response loss. If the result cannot be established or the record has expired, retain an unresolved state and route to authorized assistance; do not infer failure or launch a new mutation. A key alone is not idempotency: intent association, outcome and all relevant state must have a crash-consistent relationship. Exact retention, proof, payload equivalence, replay behavior and transport remain open for engineering proposal and Product Owner/security/privacy review.

## Failure and recovery analysis

| Scenario | Required safe behavior and recovery to evaluate | Residual concern |
|---|---|---|
| Two requests read one remaining unit or an empty duplicate range | Final atomic boundary detects the conflict, permits only a valid winner and returns a truthful safe result for others; rollback all losing effects. Test missing-row/range races as well as a shared row. | Incomplete predicate coverage or an alternate write path can still overbook. |
| Resource overlap, cross-slot reschedule or simultaneous policy change | Evaluate service capacity, all affected resource intervals and effective policy coherently. Reject or resolve a changed configuration under its authorized impact contract; on reschedule rejection preserve the old allocation. | Lock order, policy versions and cross-resource contention can cause deadlock or leave a race if a path bypasses the boundary. |
| Deadlock, serialization abort or timeout before established commit | Roll back atomically; distinguish a definitely aborted attempt from an unknown outcome. A controlled retry is safe only after prior outcome and same-intent relationship are established and authority/policy remain applicable. | A timeout is not inherently a rollback; automatic retries can amplify contention or mask an uncertain commit. |
| Commit succeeds but response is lost, process crashes, or client repeats | Report unknown until authorized reconciliation retrieves the established outcome; concurrent repeats of the same intent cannot create another mutation. Never treat email delivery as proof or repeat trigger. | Lost/expired correlation and unavailable records may require human review; availability and retention decisions are open. |
| Required evidence write fails or disagrees with committed outcome | Prevent a success claim without required consistent evidence; the eventual design must show how failure is atomic or otherwise safely resolved under the approved evidence contract. Alert and investigate without exposing personal data. | Separate stores or asynchronous recording can create gaps; an alert is not attributable audit evidence. |
| Store/worker outage, failover or restore | Stop or safely reject/hold new writes where integrity cannot be established; reconcile uncertain outcomes against authoritative state before follow-up. Reconcile restored bookings, allocations, operation outcomes and required evidence together under an authorized recovery process. | Restore may lose acknowledged state depending on the eventual recovery profile; no recovery objective or supported deployment has been approved. |
| Unauthorized lookup, duplicate probing or replay | Apply actor/service/action authorization and appointment-scoped guest proof, minimize response detail and record authorized security evidence. A warning must not disclose unrelated appointments. | Correlation identifiers and matching data can become tracking or enumeration vectors; qualified security/privacy review is still required. |

## Conditional engineering direction and decision ownership

Evaluate a transaction-centered design with enforceable database constraints where they precisely match approved policy, complemented by explicit concurrency control for remaining predicates and durable same-intent reconciliation. This is a **conditional evaluation direction**, not a selection: favor it only if the chosen store can demonstrate complete atomic coverage of policy, capacity, resource, duplicate, outcome and required evidence, and if operational owners can support contention and recovery. Compare it against optimistic/serializable and ordered-processing alternatives with realistic cross-resource scenarios before recommending a concrete combination. Do not use a queue or a fixed unique constraint as a substitute for demonstrating the full invariants.

Engineering should produce a store-specific proof and failure model after policy inputs and supported environment are known. Product Owner disposition is required for material risk/scope choices; security, privacy and data-integrity reviewers assess authority, disclosure, retention and correctness; the actual operations owner assesses monitoring and recovery feasibility. SDD-006 exact-version approval and resolved relevant upstream policy inputs precede affected implementation; other applicable spec approvals, qualified reviews and separate implementation/test authorization remain necessary. Unresolved residual risks must be presented, not assumed accepted.

## Proposed verification criteria — not executed

In an independently isolated test environment, use approved synthetic data and record actual versions, configuration, policy and duplicate preconditions, concurrency orchestration, observations, reviewer, evidence location and limitations. Establish the oracle from authoritative persisted booking, allocations, same-intent outcomes and required evidence, not only HTTP responses. Exercise all supported paths and direct/alternative writers.

1. Execute D-070 with 50 simultaneous valid distinct requests against one remaining capacity unit, explicitly configuring duplicate preconditions so all 50 are eligible competitors. Verify exactly one committed reservation, 49 safe clear unsuccessful outcomes, and zero capacity, prohibited-duplicate, partial or orphaned violations. Repeat with guest/account and permitted staff/proxy paths as applicable; this verifies correctness, not latency.
2. Force interleavings for overlapping exclusive/shared resources, configured service capacity, duplicate-window boundaries, concurrent policy changes and permitted warning/block cases. Verify all constraints together, legitimate proxy/multiple-attendee cases, no ordinary blocking bypass and no unrelated disclosure.
3. Inject failures before and after each relevant write and commit, including required evidence failure, deadlock, serialization conflict and timeout. Inspect durable state after rollback and restart. Force response loss after commit, concurrent same-intent replay, mismatched payload, expired correlation and unauthorized lookup; confirm truthful outcomes and no blind duplicate mutation.
4. Race rescheduling and cancellation with new bookings and configuration changes. A successful reschedule atomically reallocates; a known rejection preserves the original; unknown remains unresolved until authoritative reconciliation. Verify inclusive cutoff/time-zone and exception authorization against approved policy.
5. Exercise authorized outage, failover and restore scenarios with coherent booking, allocation, intent and evidence recovery; independently assess access, minimization, retention, monitoring and operational procedures. Record unresolved gaps and obtain applicable review rather than treating a passing contention test as complete security or recovery assurance.

No current result, performance target, security/privacy finding, release readiness or operational assurance is asserted by these proposed tests.
