# TECH-WP-01 — Track B: Identity and security evaluation

> **Evaluation draft — 2026-11-13.** Comparative engineering input for Product Owner and independent security, privacy and accessibility review. No factor, provider, role matrix, proof or recovery protocol is selected or approved; no implementation, live processing, test execution or release is authorized. This document is not an SDD or requirements amendment.

## Authority and scope

The effective [NFR-003 v0.9 and AC-027–029](../requirements/REQUIREMENTS-SPECIFICATION-v0.9.md), read with v0.8/v0.7, require MFA before protected internal access, accessible individually attributable paths without personal devices, controlled lifecycle and independently authorized sole-super-admin recovery. D-103/104/105/108 in the [Decision Register](../decisions/DECISION_REGISTER.md) preserve guest independence, privacy, MFA and default-deny authority. D-104's Philippines initial context does not decide legal roles, fields, retention or provider. Customer accounts and public guests are outside D-105's internal MFA coverage; this does not waive their independent appointment proof. D-102 requires proportionate comparison, and D-098 separates SDD approval from implementation authority.

[SDD-002 v0.5](../specs/SDD-002-INTERNAL-IDENTITY-PROVISIONING-AND-PERMISSION-ENFORCEMENT.md) and [SDD-008 v0.2](../specs/SDD-008-APPOINTMENT-SCOPED-GUEST-ACCESS-AND-ASSISTED-RECOVERY.md) are drafts pending review; their prior v0.4 and v0.1 respectively retain their exact approved scope. [SDD-013 v0.3](../specs/SDD-013-CONTESTED-ACTION-HOLDS-AND-ASSIGNED-CASE-REVIEW.md) is draft. Consult [SDD-003](../specs/SDD-003-REQUIRED-AUDIT-AND-OPERATIONAL-EVIDENCE.md), SDD-010/011 and SDD-024 for evidence, customer accounts/linking and privacy boundaries; draft text is not approval. This evaluation responds to the P0 identity comparison in [TASKS.md](../../TASKS.md), not to a selection request. Named reviewers and actual client operators remain unassigned.

## Candidate comparisons

| Area and candidate | Benefit and burden | Failure, mitigation and residual risk | Evidence needed before selection |
|---|---|---|---|
| Internal MFA: individually assigned hardware cryptographic authenticator, with suitable activation factor where required | Can provide phishing-resistant authentication and a path without a personal phone; procurement, replacement, compatible ports/readers, accessible operation and secure custody cost money and staff time. | Loss, theft, inaccessible controls or device incompatibility locks out users; assess accessible alternatives, revocation and controlled replacement. Physical possession and phishing resistance do not eliminate endpoint compromise or social engineering. | Demonstrate actual factor composition, origin binding, accessible enrollment/use/recovery on supported devices, individual attribution, loss and replacement; verify costs and procurement. |
| Internal MFA: password plus individually assigned hardware OTP device or accessible software OTP on an organization-provided device | Potentially familiar and usable without a personal device; requires device provisioning, seed protection, clock/operational support and accessible code entry. | OTP can be relayed by phishing; compromised seed or endpoint can undermine it. Assess anti-phishing training, restricted enrollment, throttling, revocation and an accessible alternative; none makes manually entered OTP phishing-resistant. | Assess device accessibility and usability, seed custody, phishing/relay exposure, loss, supported environments and lifecycle; compare against cryptographic option in realistic workflows. |
| Internal MFA: personal-phone push/OTP or SMS | Familiar for some users, but personal-device dependence, connectivity, telecom/provider and device-access barriers create exclusion and operational dependencies. | Social engineering, compromised phone or number transfer may defeat the path; outages and travel can deny access. It cannot be the only route under AC-028; evaluate independently assigned alternatives and recovery. | Actual provider terms and processing, device availability, accessibility, loss, number change, compromise and offline behavior; no vendor or delivery service is assumed. |
| Guest proof: independently issued, appointment-scoped secret created at booking and protected by the booker | Can allow account-free management without a customer account; issuance, safe storage, accessibility, loss and replay protection add burden. | Copy, theft or interception can transfer practical control; a reference/QR, email possession or personal-data match alone is explicitly insufficient. Assess whether the proposed secret is truly independent of those channels and how it is issued and bounded; refer lost/uncertain proof to authorized assistance. | Threat model for issuance and channel correlation, scope and replay, accessibility, disclosure and recovery; test cross-appointment denial. A secret candidate is not approved proof. |
| Guest proof: independent assisted verification by separately authorized staff | Provides a route when online proof is unavailable; client staffing, training, availability and review create burden and privacy risk. | Impersonation, staff collusion, mistaken matches and excessive document collection remain possible. Require reviewed independent evidence, assigned action scope, attribution and escalation; no universal ID-document collection. | PO/service-policy decision on permissible evidence and operations; privacy/security review, realistic abuse, misidentification and access-limited handling tests. A matching email or demographic detail alone cannot suffice. |
| Guest proof: email link, reference/QR or personal-data matching alone | Easy to use and operate, but each alone conflicts with D-103 and NFR-003. | Shared mailbox, leaked reference or known personal facts can lead to disclosure or takeover. These may have a limited supporting role only within later approved design; none alone establishes booker authority. | Reject as sole authority; evaluate channel independence and permitted six-purpose email boundaries before any combined approach. |
| Authorization: role grants plus actor/service/action checks | Familiar operational grouping with explicit server-side default denial; maintain a reviewed matrix and scoped assignments. | Broad roles or a missing service check can expose records; test direct requests and cross-service and cross-installation denial. Role names alone do not establish permissions; revocation propagation remains a design issue. | PO-approved assignment and sensitive-action matrix, threat review, automated negative checks, revocation and audit access tests. |
| Authorization: policy/attribute-based decisions alongside scoped roles | Can express context and exception boundaries more precisely; policies, precedence, data quality and operational debugging increase complexity. | Missing or stale attributes, permissive fallback or inconsistent policy engines can grant access; fail closed and review change control. Complexity itself can produce errors. | Compare with scoped role approach on actual service/action cases, missing attributes, conflict precedence, maintenance burden and denial tests. No policy engine or package is selected. |
| Contested actions: assigned trained reviewer accepts and applies a narrow hold | Protects the disputed action or individual link while preserving appointment and permitted attendance; requires coverage, escalation and case handling. | Malicious claims or staff mistakes can interrupt service or disclose data. Failed or uncertain proof alone does not cause a hold; no claimant self-release, automatic cancellation or implicit reversal. Distinguish review, release and reversal permissions. | Resolve CASE-Q01–Q06, including acceptance threshold, conflict handling, evidence, coverage and unknown outcomes. Test assigned/unassigned actors, unrelated actions, attendance, no automatic cancellation and separate reversal authority. |
| Contested actions: automatic hold on every failed proof or broad appointment lock | Simple trigger and apparent caution, but contradicts D-103/108 and risks denial of service and disruption to attendance. | Attackers can repeatedly fail verification to block others. Do not select; a narrow authorized-review approach still has human error and delay risks. | Rejection follows policy; evaluate abuse resistance and measurable operational workload for any permissible alternative. |
| Sole-super-admin recovery: independently authorized client-controlled procedure | Can restore administration without standing developer/operator access; needs a real independent authorizer, availability, identity checks, controlled execution and handover in each installation. | Collusion, forged authorization or loss of the only independent authorizer risks takeover or lockout. Pending/uncertain recovery grants no access; controlled evidence, revocation and post-recovery review are needed. | PO and security review the independent authority, substitution/escalation and evidence; test one-admin installation, absent authorizer, forged requests, factor loss, session invalidation and no standing operator access. |
| Recovery: pre-enrolled recovery credentials held by the same super-admin | Convenient and may reduce delays, but possession by the same individual alone does not satisfy independently authorized sole-admin recovery. Stored credentials can be stolen or lost. | They may be considered only as a component of a separately authorized process after review; they cannot establish independent authorization or an unrestricted bypass. | Demonstrate independence and custody, expiry/revocation and compromise handling; verify access remains denied without independent authorization. |
| Recovery: standing developer/operator override or shared emergency account | Appears fast but conflicts with client authority, attribution and D-105/D-108. | Persistent backdoor or shared credential can expose all records and obscure responsibility. Exceptional support requires explicit client authorization and scoped, attributable closure; it is not a default override. | Reject standing override/shared credentials. Test denied operator access, separately authorized scoped support, expiry and closure. |

## Conditional direction and unresolved decisions

For further evaluation, prioritize an individually assigned phishing-resistant cryptographic option **and** an accessible non-personal-device pathway, but select neither until devices, accessibility, cost, lifecycle and independent recovery are demonstrated. Hardware OTP can be a comparison or alternative only if its phishing and accessibility risks are accepted through the applicable process; no single candidate shown proves all users can use it. [NIST SP 800-63B-4](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html) distinguishes cryptographic phishing resistance from manually entered OTP; it is external technical guidance, not an adopted iTakda assurance level. [W3C WCAG 2.2 accessible authentication](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/) informs evaluation, not evidence of compliance.

For guests, investigate independently issued scoped proof plus separately controlled assisted recovery, subject to proof of independence and service-specific authority. Linking also requires authenticated account control, independent proof, explicit confirmation and audit; it grants only the individually authorized appointment, not attendee or representative authority. Distinguish booker, attendee and proxy/guardian contexts; uncertain claims are denied or referred. Do not assume issuance by email makes an email-only proof independent, or infer an approved notification purpose. SDD-007/D-095 must be checked before any message is designed.

Start authorization evaluation with a small actor/service/action matrix and default denial, comparing any contextual policy mechanism against it. Separate case review, hold release, reversal, audit viewing and export permissions; do not infer self-elevation, super-admin appointment or business-data access from a title or infrastructure custody. Additional super-admin appointment needs independent approval and fresh verification without self-approval. The actual matrix, first custodian and independent recovery authority require PO decisions; qualified security, accessibility and privacy reviewers assess them before affected implementation. Assess actual hosting model, client staffing, device costs and provider terms; no estimates or service commitments are asserted.

## Future verification and decision evidence

Before selection, reviewers should document threat assumptions and realistic attack/recovery scenarios, each candidate's accessibility and individual attribution, costs and support responsibilities, and residual risks, including stolen proof, phishing, insider abuse and unavailability. Privacy review should assess minimum necessary data, notice/purpose, provider processing, evidence access and lifecycle, including backup/restored restrictions. Do not log factor secrets, recovery codes or unnecessary claimant data. Retention, legal roles and actual operator responsibilities remain open under D-104 and qualified review.

Under separately authorized design and testing, exercise covered internal roles, protected access before/after MFA, inaccessible or missing personal devices, failed/uncertain checks, enrollment and factor replacement, revocation/deactivation and affected session invalidation. Test independent additional-admin approval and sole-admin recovery with unavailable or fraudulent authorizers and no operator backdoor. Exercise guest cross-appointment denial, stolen/shared proof, linking confirmation and representative uncertainty. Test service/action and direct-request denial, self-elevation, assigned/unassigned reviewer and hold/reversal separation; preserve appointment and permitted attendance. Test accessible keyboard, assistive technology and recovery journeys, neutral denials, outcome-consistent restricted evidence and separate export permission. Reconcile pending/unknown outcomes without claiming success. These are proposed tests, not executed results or new normative criteria.

**Decision and implementation prerequisites:** PO policy and exact applicable SDD/interface approvals, resolved IDN/GUEST/CASE questions and permission assignments, qualified security/privacy/accessibility review, independent installation-specific recovery and operations ownership, and separate high-risk implementation authorization. No evaluation result here establishes legal compliance, security assurance, accepted testing or Gate 2/Checkpoint 11 completion.
