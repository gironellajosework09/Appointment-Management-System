## Audit and diagnostics candidates

| Candidate | Benefit and cost | Failure, privacy and recovery considerations |
|---|---|---|
| Required evidence written within the same local transactional boundary as the owning state change | Can make an established outcome and required evidence consistent, if the eventual store supports the entire boundary. Adds write/storage load and couples availability of required evidence and the command. | Evidence failure before commit must not yield an established success or partial state. Lost responses still need authoritative reconciliation. Transaction semantics, durability, tamper resistance and restored access restrictions require proof; a transaction alone does not provide independent tamper protection. |
| Separate evidence store or asynchronous event delivery | Can isolate workloads and support separate custody; adds integration, operations, reconciliation and potential licensing/hosting cost. | A crash or network outage between business commit and evidence persistence can leave a gap. It is acceptable to evaluate only if the design demonstrates how required evidence remains consistent, including partial failures and recovery; a best-effort later log alone is insufficient. Separate custody does not automatically confer authorization or immutability. |
| Local append-oriented evidence with restricted modification, versus independently protected archive/integrity checks | Local controls may be simpler to operate; separate protection may improve detection or limit a single administrator's ability to alter history, at further custody, capacity and restoration cost. | Neither naming a log append-only nor using a checksum proves tamper resistance against a privileged actor. Evaluate actual threat boundaries, key/custody and correction provenance, detection, availability, retention and restore. No WORM, hash, archive or vendor is selected. |
| Protected operational diagnostics, separate in purpose and reader scope from audit | Minimum useful signals can aid diagnosis without duplicating business records; filtering, capacity monitoring and access administration require maintenance. | Public failures remain neutral; diagnostics must not expose proof secrets, credentials or unnecessary personal information. Diagnostic rotation cannot delete mandatory audit evidence. Review access, safe redaction, outage, disk/inode exhaustion and restoration. An ordinary diagnostic failure is not automatically equivalent to loss of required audit evidence. |

Viewing and exporting require separate attributable authorization; neither technical custody nor a broad administrator label grants unrestricted client data. Review list, detail, filter and direct access by installation/action/record scope; viewing does not confer export, correction, disposal or business-command rights. Exact assignments, event catalog, export conditions and retention remain open. Any necessary exception, reconciliation or correction must preserve attributable context and distinguish prior and subsequent outcomes; not every read or denial is presumed to need a new stored record. Qualified privacy review must address necessary content, permitted readers, retention/disposition and restored copies before affected processing.

### Outcome and evidence failure matrix

| Situation | Required interpretation and evaluation |
|---|---|
| Business action and required evidence established | Report the established outcome only; verify evidence identifies the applicable actor, scope and outcome under the consuming contract. |
| Rejection or required evidence unavailable before completion | Do not claim success or leave partial business effects. Test the candidate boundary; separately required security/rejection evidence follows its own reviewed policy. |
| Response lost or commit state uncertain | Do not infer success or failure from the browser; reconcile against authoritative state without blind reissue. Avoid promising rollback after a commit or external effect. |
| Evidence store unavailable/full or archive/restore fails | Expose the actual operational condition to authorized responders; evaluate capacity safeguards and command-specific uncertainty. Never silently discard mandatory evidence or bypass access controls. |
| Unauthorized view or export | Deny server-side, including direct requests and cross-installation attempts. Separate export from view; neutral public responses must not disclose protected diagnostics. |

## Notification candidates

| Candidate | Benefit and trade-off | Failure and recovery assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Direct after-commit dispatch with no durable intent | Few moving parts and lower initial operating burden. | A process crash after commit but before dispatch can lose an eligible message; a timeout after submission leaves ambiguous provider acceptance. Alone it cannot demonstrate reliable independent recovery. A separately justified reconciliation mechanism would be needed. |
| Persisted notification intent coordinated with the authoritative commit, then independently processed | Can preserve eligible work across process/worker outages and decouple mail from the committed appointment; introduces durable state, storage, worker operations, duplicate handling and privacy/retention obligations. | A crash around provider submission may leave the send outcome uncertain. Retrying must not repeat the business command; duplicate-message controls depend on actual transport/provider capabilities and cannot promise exactly-once delivery. Evaluate safe reconciliation and restricted access. No outbox or queue is selected. |
| Reconstruct eligible notifications by reconciliation against authoritative records | May recover omissions without depending solely on a worker; adds scans, decision rules, processing cost and retention/privacy exposure. | Reconstruction must distinguish already submitted, uncertain and unsent work; otherwise it can duplicate mail or use outdated recipients/content. Test whether sufficient authoritative evidence exists and its access is justified. It is not a substitute for a demonstrated reliable boundary. |

A replaceable delivery interface is a candidate to reduce provider lock-in, but does not standardize different provider semantics or remove migration, credential, security and testing costs. Compare direct delivery against a managed provider only at selection time, using current official terms and technical documentation: processing locations and subprocessors, data access/retention, message and event availability, credentials, throttling, costs/quotas, incident duties and exit/migration. Qualified privacy/security review and actual installation responsibility allocation precede adoption; no provider, legal conclusion or cost figure is assumed.

### Delivery and recovery failure matrix

| Situation | Boundary and candidate verification |
|---|---|
| Committed transaction; browser response lost | Authoritative commit can support its permitted notification even without a browser response. Reconcile the same intent; do not repeat the booking or suppress a legitimate eligible notification solely because the response was lost. |
| Unknown, uncommitted, rejected or held transaction | Uncertainty alone does not authorize a transaction success message. Resolve authoritative state and recipient/content eligibility first. |
| Database or worker unavailable; process crashes around commit or dispatch | Determine whether eligible intent was durably established, reconcile gaps and resume notification separately. A committed appointment remains committed; never retry the business action to repair email. |
| Provider outage, throttling, timeout or ambiguous acknowledgement | Preserve the established outcome, use reviewed bounded recovery and report only known status. A timeout can leave submission uncertain; indiscriminate retry may duplicate messages. No retry count, delay or delivery guarantee is adopted. |
| Duplicate, delayed or out-of-order processing; bounce or other provider signal | Check eligibility against authoritative outcome and current approved recipient/content rules. Distinguish duplicate attempts from confirmed separate messages; do not infer inbox receipt, reading or identity from a provider signal. Exact event interpretation depends on actual provider evidence. |
| Credential compromise or unauthorized access | Contain and rotate/revoke through an approved operational procedure, restrict processing and investigate under assigned incident authority; do not log secrets or expose recipient/content unnecessarily. Custody and incident responsibilities remain open. |

Status vocabulary must distinguish at least what is established about the business action from what is actually known about notification processing. A request, local acceptance or provider acceptance is not proof of inbox delivery. Unknown and failure states must remain understandable and provide a safe next action; the exact vocabulary is not selected. Confirmation on screen/print remains authoritative for the established transaction.

## Conditional direction and evidence required

**Recommendation for further evaluation, not a selection:** prioritize a locally coordinated required-evidence boundary and a recoverable, persisted notification-intent candidate, provided the eventual technology can prove its transaction and recovery properties. Compare them with the alternatives above against the actual supported installation profiles and constraints. Separate protected diagnostics from mandatory audit and keep any delivery integration replaceable where this is proportionate. If feasibility, privacy, availability or custody evidence does not support these candidates, reassess rather than treating this direction as approval.

Before affected implementation approval, engineering and security/data-integrity reviewers should document the boundary and interleavings, failure handling, credible costs and residual risks; qualified privacy reviewers should evaluate necessary data, provider processing, notice, retention and restored restrictions. The Product Owner resolves material trade-offs and provider/commitment choices. Installation/client and provider operators must be assigned real responsibilities before reliance. EVD-Q01–Q04 and MAIL-Q01–Q04 remain open; no numeric retention, throughput, retry, availability, RPO or RTO value is inferred.

Proposed verification, only after separate authorization and against the final approved design:

1. Inject failures before/after commit and during evidence persistence; verify no partial state, truthful unknown outcomes and authoritative reconciliation, including tamper/correction and capacity/restore scenarios.
2. Exercise authorized and unauthorized viewer/export and direct endpoint access, cross-installation isolation, neutral failures, minimization and absence of credentials/proof secrets; test retained and restored restrictions.
3. Cover all six purposes and consumer integrations, recipient/content restrictions, committed/rejected/held/unknown outcomes, lost browser response, worker/database/provider outages, throttling, duplicate and out-of-order attempts, bounce/uncertain signals and credential incident handling. Confirm notification recovery never reissues the business command.
4. Verify status and recovery with applicable automated and human keyboard, screen-reader, zoom and touch review. Record environments, expected versus observed results, evidence owners and limitations; test doubles do not demonstrate real provider delivery or inbox receipt.

This document records candidate analysis and proposed verification only. No test, actual delivery, tamper resistance, accessibility conformance, privacy/security review, operational readiness or residual-risk acceptance is claimed.
