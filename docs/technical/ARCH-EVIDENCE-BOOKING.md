# Booking conflict and failure model — proposed architecture evidence

**Status: investigation draft, 2026-11-13.** This is a concrete model to review, not a selected architecture, physical schema, SQL, implementation or test result. It develops the direction investigated in [TECH-WP-02 booking](TECH-WP-02-BOOKING.md) under [D-102 and Engineering Governance](../ENGINEERING_GOVERNANCE.md). Any endorsed direction remains authorization to investigate only. No application, database, dependency, test, Git or deployment action is authorized or claimed here. Engineering/architecture proposes the model; a qualified data-integrity/security reviewer and applicable privacy reviewer must assess it. Product Owner decides material policy and risk; actual reviewers and operations owners remain unassigned.

The proposals below do not close INT-Q01–Q07 or choose capacity units, intervals, duplicate identity/window, exception types, retention, API, store version, isolation or roles.

The invariant is one coherent final decision for all authorized guest, account, staff, proxy, walk-in, reschedule and other booking-affecting paths: current authority and effective policy, capacity, all resource constraints, appointment state and duplicate policy must agree. Availability is advisory. Required evidence must agree with committed outcome. A rejected reschedule leaves its old allocation intact. Unknown outcome is neither success nor rejection and requires same-intent reconciliation before follow-up. Configuration effects on accepted appointments remain under SDD-022; email is outside the booking transaction. D-106–108 provide policy and authority boundaries, not a locking design.

## Proposed conflict domains

Each domain is a **logical set of potentially conflicting operations**, not a table or selected key. If two operations could make the same invariant false, their protections must overlap; a narrower mapping is invalid. Preliminary domains:

| Domain | Must cover | Example conflict |
|---|---|---|
| Installation policy/authority epoch | Effective service, capacity, resource, duplicate, cutoff and relevant authorization policy revisions and their activation; configuration writers must participate. | Booking evaluated under an old limit races the effective change. The SDD-022 impact decision is separate; an implementation cannot bypass it merely by locking. |
| Appointment state | The existing appointment for cancel, reschedule, exception or another approved state change, including any associated hold and eligibility state needed for the action. | Cancellation and reschedule cannot both act on the same prior allocation. |
| Service capacity | Every capacity unit/period the operation consumes or releases, including all periods touched by approved duration/buffer semantics. | Two otherwise valid requests see one remaining unit. |
| Staff/physical resources | Every exclusive or shared resource and every time interval whose occupancy could overlap, including preparation/cleanup buffers. | Different service slots overlap on the same staff member; a slot-only lock misses it. |
| Duplicate policy | Approved attendee/eligibility criterion, service and every possible comparison interval that can overlap the applicable window, including both sides of a reschedule. | Two first bookings have no existing matching row, yet both would violate blocking policy. Booker contact alone is never the criterion. |
| Command intent | The authorized command identity and its bound payload/actor scope and outcome, including concurrent repetitions. | Two deliveries of one intent must not create two bookings; same identifier with a different payload cannot silently become the first command. |
| Evidence/outcome | Required attributable evidence and committed command outcome tied to the actual mutation, with any required unique appointment reference. | An accepted booking and required evidence cannot disagree after a partial failure. |

Exact mapping must be derived from approved policy. In particular, arbitrary overlapping intervals and moving duplicate windows cannot be safely represented by a single start-time bucket unless **every** pair that can conflict shares at least one protected domain. Adjacent buckets, window boundaries, midnight/time-zone transitions, multiple resources and changing policies require proof. False sharing merely increases contention; a missing shared domain permits a violation. No raw personal identifier should appear in lock names, diagnostics or externally visible responses; any derivation requires privacy and collision analysis.

## Proposed locking protocol to investigate

For a first correctness model, consider a deliberately coarse, pre-existing **installation integrity gate** for every booking-affecting mutation and policy activation. Holding it for the final database decision makes all such operations conflict, including empty ranges and dynamically discovered domains. It is a conceptual safety baseline, not a required global lock or a selection. It requires one authoritative transactional store/connection and universal participation, including imports, maintenance and recovery writers; exceptions to that assumption require separate controls. It may serialize an entire installation and impose unacceptable waiting or availability cost. The actual throughput and operation profile are unknown and must be assessed only under separate test authorization.

If a finer protocol is proposed, prove domain coverage against the coarse baseline before reducing the gate's scope. A candidate ordering is: (1) integrity/policy coordination, (2) command intent, (3) existing appointment, (4) service capacity domains, (5) resource domains, (6) duplicate domains, then (7) outcome/evidence writes. Within each class use a stable canonical ordering based on non-sensitive internal identifiers and interval order, taking the union of old and new domains for a reschedule. Every conflicting configuration and mutation path must acquire the same classes in the same order; merely sorting within one workflow is insufficient. Evidence/reference uniqueness constraints may themselves lock internally, so the actual full lock graph must be examined. Deterministic ordering reduces, but does not eliminate, deadlocks.

**Discovery problem:** domain membership may depend on changing policy, resources or the current appointment. Do not read a list, release protection, and assume it remains complete. Hold the coarse gate while deriving and acquiring the complete set, or use a separately proven coordination/version protocol that prevents an omitted domain from changing before commit. If the operation discovers an earlier-ordered or additional domain after acquisition, abort the attempt, establish rollback and restart discovery in canonical order; do not acquire it out of order. If a complete conflict set cannot be enumerated, retain a safely broad domain or stop the affected action. A fine-grained design must also serialize authorization/policy changes or verify a stable authoritative revision at commit; an early authorization check alone may go stale.

**Absent rows:** an empty search result is not a lockable logical absence by itself. Under the coarse gate all participating writers serialize even when there is no reservation or duplicate row. A finer model could use pre-existing protected guard domains, or atomically create a uniquely identified guard and resolve simultaneous creation before evaluating; both require a non-null collision-free mapping, reliable creation/cleanup lifecycle, full predicate coverage and actual store-specific proof. Gap/predicate locking may be evaluated against the exact version, isolation, query and indexes; [MariaDB's locking documentation](https://mariadb.com/docs/server/reference/sql-statements/administrative-sql-statements/set-commands/set-transaction) does not make an arbitrary empty-range query safe at every isolation level. Guard expiration, policy changes or restore must never allow two guards for the same conflict domain. None of these alternatives is selected.

## Transaction and interface model

1. **Admission:** obtain an authenticated/authorized actor or independently established appointment-scoped guest authority as applicable. Accept an operation identity only under a reviewed same-intent contract; an appointment reference alone is not proof. Validate syntax without treating pre-displayed availability as a reservation. No personally identifying error detail is exposed.
2. **Final transaction:** begin an explicit transaction over transaction-safe state, obtain the proposed conflict protection and establish or reconcile the intent association. Re-read effective policy, authority evidence/version, appointment state, capacity, resources and duplicate conditions under the protected decision. If required state cannot be established consistently, do not report success. Policy warnings require the approved accessible acknowledgement and must still pass other checks; exceptions require the specific permission and attributable reason.
3. **Mutation and evidence:** create/change appointment and allocations, record its authoritative outcome and required attributable evidence in the same atomic boundary, subject to SDD-003's eventual contract. For reschedule, old release and new reservation are one transaction. Constraints may enforce precisely expressible invariants, but are not a substitute for the complete policy checks. Do not perform external email delivery inside this boundary. If evidence resides elsewhere, an alternative consistency protocol requires its own proof and approval; this model assumes no such mechanism has been selected.
4. **Commit and response:** return success only on an established commit with required evidence; return known rejection only when its result and no partial effects are established. If a meaningful rejection must be replayable, the intent contract must explain whether and how its outcome is durably recorded without committing partial booking effects. An email failure cannot reverse or repeat a booking. Notifications consume only the authoritative committed outcome under their own contract.

The interfaces with identity/guest proof (SDD-002/008), policy and eligibility (SDD-004/005), evidence (SDD-003), notifications (SDD-007), rescheduling (SDD-017), exceptions (SDD-018), configuration impacts (SDD-022), and accessible outcomes (SDD-023) are logical responsibilities, not approved APIs. Each must specify freshness, error/unknown behavior and ownership before integration. If authority or policy is supplied by another independent system, the single-store atomicity assumption fails unless a separately reviewed consistency protocol proves equivalent outcomes.

## Intent and failure state model

Proposed logical states are **not established**, **in progress**, **committed**, **known rejected/aborted**, and **unknown to the caller**. Unknown describes knowledge, not an additional booking status: it may cover an in-flight, committed or aborted transaction. Same-intent association must be scoped to authorized access, bind the intended payload, serialize concurrent uses, distinguish an altered payload, and survive the same relevant failure and recovery boundaries as the booking. Token source, payload equivalence, expiry and retention are open. Where an authorized lookup finds an established outcome, return that outcome; if it cannot prove one, remain unresolved and route to authorized assistance. Absence after expiry or restore is not proof of non-commit. A new intent is a fresh decision against current policy; do not disguise an unknown prior command as a new one.

| Interleaving/failure | Proposed result and recovery | Evidence gap |
|---|---|---|
| Two distinct requests, one unit; neither has an existing booking row | Both reach the same capacity domain/gate. After first commits, second reads current protected state and receives a safe unsuccessful outcome. | Prove all paths and absent-row coordination; do not infer success from lock naming. |
| Same intent sent simultaneously, or identifier reused with different payload | One association owns the intent; matching repeats reconcile its outcome, different payload is rejected as an identity conflict without exposing another person's record. If still in flight or unavailable, report unresolved. | Payload binding, proof and behavior after expiration require policy/security review. |
| Opposing reschedules A→B and B→A | Protect union of both old/new capacity and resource domains in the same order; re-read appointment and policy. Either a valid atomic outcome or known rejection preserving the original is produced. | Domain completeness, lock-order interactions and starvation require verification. |
| Configuration changes while booking is in progress | Common policy gate or proven revision check orders activation with the final booking decision. SDD-022 handles impacts on existing bookings; no silent cancellation. | Exact policy effective-time and impact outcomes remain open. |
| Deadlock victim or lock-wait timeout | Establish transaction status; explicitly roll back a still-active transaction, including any earlier writes. Once definitely aborted, a controlled same-intent follow-up may re-evaluate current authority/policy. | [MariaDB documents](https://mariadb.com/docs/server/server-usage/storage-engines/innodb/innodb-system-variables) that a timeout normally rolls back the statement rather than the whole transaction, unlike a deadlock victim; confirm exact release/settings. |
| Connection loss before or during commit, or lost response after commit | Treat caller outcome as unknown; use authorized same-intent reconciliation against authoritative state. Do not infer failure from timeout, rollback locally by assumption, issue another mutation or use email as proof. | Availability, durable intent association and restored-state completeness remain unproved. |
| Allocation/evidence write fails before commit | Roll back all affected booking state and establish whether rollback succeeded; no success or orphan is reported. If rollback/commit state cannot be established, keep outcome unknown. | Transaction-safe engine, failure semantics and evidence co-location are unselected. |
| Crash, restore or data divergence | Restrict affected actions until authorized reconciliation of booking, allocation, intent and evidence; use reviewed recovery procedures and record unresolved gaps. | Commit durability and backup loss window depend on actual configuration; no recovery objective, owner or operational evidence is approved. |

## Optimistic alternative

An alternative reads the same policy, appointment and all conflict-domain versions, then conditionally updates **all** of them in one atomic transaction before committing effects. A conflict causes full rollback and re-evaluation, not partial update. A domain for an absent row must already exist or have an independently proven atomic creation protocol. A single slot counter cannot detect cross-slot resource overlap, shifting duplicate windows or policy activation. Discovery, authority and rescheduling still need a complete union of domains and coherent versions; checking each version in separate transactions does not provide atomicity. With complete domains it may reduce held-lock time under low contention, but adds version/guard maintenance, repeated work, starvation and conflict storms. Outcome reconciliation is still mandatory after uncertain commit. Compare it with the coarse and finer locking candidates against identical workloads and failure cases; none is selected, and no speed claim is made.

## Decisions and future verification

Product Owner must resolve applicable policy values, exception scope and material risk; engineering must define and prove conflict mapping, authority/policy freshness, intent scope and transaction/evidence boundaries; data-integrity and security reviewers must assess race and abuse cases; privacy reviewers must assess identifiers, diagnostics and retention; actual operators must assess contention, incident response and recovery. Exact-version SDD approval, upstream contracts, specialist review, and separate implementation/test/processing/release authorization remain applicable. No residual risk is accepted here.

When separately authorized, use synthetic data in an isolated environment and record actual version, engine, indexes/query plans, isolation, autocommit, settings, policy preconditions, orchestration, persisted state, evidence and reviewer. Verify:

1. **D-070/AC-005:** 50 valid simultaneous distinct requests for one remaining capacity unit, with duplicate-policy preconditions that allow all to compete: exactly one committed reservation, 49 safe clear unsuccessful outcomes, no over-capacity, prohibited duplicate, partial or orphaned state. Inspect authoritative state and required evidence; this is a future integrity obligation, not a measured result or latency/availability SLA.
2. Empty-row and boundary interleavings for duplicate windows and overlapping exclusive/shared resources, including adjacent partitions, buffers, midnight/time-zone transitions and all supported paths; demonstrate every conflicting pair shares protection. Verify permitted proxy and warning cases without unrelated disclosure.
3. Reschedule swaps, cancellation races, policy activation, authorization changes and domain discovery changes; force deadlock/timeout and confirm full transaction cleanup, preservation of old allocation on known rejection and no out-of-order retry.
4. Failure injection at every mutation/evidence/commit boundary; response loss and concurrent same-intent replay, payload mismatch, expired/lost identifier, unauthorized lookup and restored outcomes. Confirm unknown remains unresolved until authoritative reconciliation, no second booking and consistent evidence.
5. Compare the coarse, finer and optimistic models using the same controlled scenarios, and separately exercise authorized crash/restore and operational access. Record failures and limitations rather than treating D-070 alone as security, privacy, durability or operational acceptance.
